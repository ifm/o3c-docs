## Framerate

The framerate of the `O3C` 3D imager is configurable from 4 to 20 Hz (default 10 Hz), and is independent from the applied settings (exposure mode and times, filters, etc.). Higher exposure times, for example, will **not** negatively impact the achievable framerate.

## Exposure Times

Exposure times are utilized to maximize the number of valid pixels in a scene. The use of multiple exposures (HDR) permits the camera to operate in “dynamic” environments that require the detection of dark and light objects at both the minimum and maximum ranges.

The proper exposure time for a pixel depends on factors such as the dynamics of the scene and whether the target is moving or stationary. For highly reflective targets or for motion, a short exposure time is best. For targets far away or with low surface reflectance  choosing high exposure time is preferable.
As such, it is common that all targets of a scene cannot be properly exposed with a single exposure time.
To reduce noise and the number of overexposed/underexposed pixels, we use three exposures for each frame. The `standard` modes provide two settable exposure times (`exposureLong` and `exposureShort`) plus a third *constant*, ultra-short exposure  (set at 20 µs) designed to help detect highly reflective targets in the very near range (~1 m). This ultra-short exposure is fixed and cannot be configured by the customer. Note that using a small ratio of exposure times helps reduce noise in transition regions (where neighboring pixels use different exposure times).


:::{note}
You can find which exposure time is used for each pixel by analyzing the confidence image as detailed [here](../ImagesDescription/confidenceImage.md).
:::

## Delay

The `Delay` parameter is not valid in `IDLE` state of the port and when `Delay` parameter is configured in `IDLE` state then the value is set back to its default(0). Please refer to [this](../triggering.md) section for more details.

## Offset

Coded modulation dictates the measurement range of the camera (for example 0 to 2 m in the 2 m [mode](../modes.md)). The `offset` parameter lets you shift that range closer to or further from the camera without changing the mode.

On the O3C, `offset` is an **integer index** (not a distance). One step equals the mode's maximum range divided by 8, so its size depends on the selected [mode](../modes.md). Each increment shifts the whole measurement range one step away from the camera, and each decrement shifts it one step closer. The `offset` index ranges from -6 to 16 (default 0) and can be changed frame by frame.

The resulting range for a given index is approximately:
- start ≈ `offset` × step
- end ≈ maximum range + `offset` × step

### Example (2 m mode)

Let's look at the following scene. Three boxes are positioned in front of the camera at about one, two, and three meters away.
![RGB view of the offset scene](resources/offset_scene.png)

We use the 2 m [mode](../modes.md) (one step equals 2 m / 8 = 0.25 m) with all other settings at default. The table below shows the point cloud for several offset indices and the resulting approximate measurement range:

| Offset index | Approximate range | Point Cloud                                                     |
| ------------ | ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| -2           | -0.5 m … 1.5 m    | ![Point cloud with offset -2](resources/offset_-05_cloud.png)   |
| 0 (default)  | 0 m … 2 m         | ![Point cloud with offset 0](resources/offset_0_cloud.png)      |
| 6            | 1.5 m … 3.5 m     | ![Point cloud with offset 6](resources/offset_15_cloud.png)     |
| 10           | 2.5 m … 4.5 m     | ![Point cloud with offset 10](resources/offset_25_cloud.png)    |

:::{note}
In the last image, where the offset index is 10 (range about 2.5–4.5 m), the noise is higher than in the other images.
This is due to the larger absolute distance from the object to the camera, with which the noise increases.
Additionally, the most robust measurement is in the middle of the measurement range, which is about 3 to 4 m in this last example. The ground in front of the box is outside the measurement range.
:::

Negative offsets bring the far end of the range closer to the camera, which can help reduce MPI artifacts or avoid artifacts caused by highly reflective objects (see [the stray light artifacts documentation](../ProcessingParams/strayLight.md)) by removing the source from the measurement range.

:::{note}
Because the underlying physics engine makes each step represent a slightly different distance (varying mainly with temperature, exposure time and [channel value](#channel-selection-and-channel-value)), the values above are approximate. To read the exact current range, use the `TOF_INFO` buffer of the ifm3d API, which reports the real-time min and max range. See the [ifm3d-examples repository](https://github.com/ifm/ifm3d-examples).
:::

## Channel selection and channel value

When several O3C cameras share a field of view, they can interfere with each other (crosstalk). To avoid this, give each camera a different **channel** (modulation frequency).

- Channel selection is `manual` only (no automatic selection).
- `channelValue` ranges from -100 to 100 (default 0) and must be set individually per camera.
- Neighboring cameras only need a channel value **difference of 2** to avoid crosstalk; a larger difference brings no further benefit.

To assign values systematically across a fleet, derive the channel value from a customer-controlled, roughly sequential identifier (for example a vehicle number), such as `mod(number, 200)`. A MAC address works too but is harder to map to evenly spread channels.
